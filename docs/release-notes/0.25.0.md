Two pieces of chrome step back. The chat's turn rail stays hidden until you
reach for it, and the right-hand pane can be put away from its own header,
not only from the keyboard.

**The turn rail appears when you point at it**

The ticks along a chat's left edge, one for each message you sent, are half
their old size and sit closer together. At rest they are not drawn at all.
Move the pointer into the left margin and they fade in, growing out of the
edge; move it away and they go again. With reduced motion turned on they
appear and disappear without animating. The strip that reveals them is the
margin the conversation already leaves empty, so it never covers text, and
it is wider than the old rail, so the shorter ticks are no harder to hit. Pointing
at a tick still previews its message, and clicking it still scrolls there.

**The right pane has a way out in its own header**

Since 0.24.0 the right pane is part of the layout from the first frame, and
Ctrl+Shift+B was the only way to hide it. The × at the right end of its tab
strip now hides it too, and so does a Hide Pane button at the end of the row
an empty pane offers. Hiding keeps the pane's tabs: Ctrl+Shift+B, or opening
a file, a browser or Changes, brings it back as you left it, and the ×'s
tooltip names the shortcut. It is called Hide rather than Close because
nothing is closed, and because Close Pane already names closing a terminal
split.

That × used to close every tab in the pane and leave the pane itself open;
that one-click close is gone, and each tab keeps its own ×. With no worktree
selected the × no longer appears. Before, it could be drawn over a tab left
behind by the worktree you had just deselected, and would have closed tabs
you could not see.
