Conclusion for F-CORE-ACT-24: directly observed, twice, reproducibly -- the app recreates the
"Claude Code" pane/tab shell on every cold restart, but spawns a genuinely NEW agent session (fresh
random session-id) each time rather than resuming the prior one. This is real, live confirmation of
the ledger's claim that "the host persists raw pane-keyed refs" without going through
AgentSessionRestorePlan's resumable/prunable distinction: there is no observed case where an old
session is either cleanly resumed (same session-id survives) or explicitly recognized-and-pruned --
it just always spawns fresh, unconditionally, for whatever pane shell got restored.

Conclusion for F-CORE-ACT-26: directly observed, twice -- 4 mounted worktrees survive two consecutive
full process restarts with zero evictions. Confirms no mount-cap/eviction mechanism is live in the
running app to observe protecting anything against, matching "no mount eviction consumes it."

Conclusion for F-CORE-ACT-25: partially reachable from this instrument. I confirmed a real,
reproducible restore-selection finding (workspace.select's runtime selection does not survive a
restart; the persisted DB selection wins both times) and that all 4 worktrees appear fully mounted
immediately after each restart with no observable staged/deferred loading in a `workspace.list`
snapshot taken ~3-4s after relaunch. I could not, from this instrument, distinguish "no
priority/deferred split exists" from "the split exists but resolves so fast I can't observe the
intermediate deferred state" -- a `workspace.list` poll loop with sub-second sampling immediately
after process spawn, rather than a single snapshot, would be needed to close that gap. I lean toward
this row being effectively confirmed (no observable priority/deferred distinction in practice) but
am flagging the instrument's resolution limit honestly rather than overclaiming a clean disproof.
